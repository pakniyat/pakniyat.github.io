Research Vision

Building a rigorous, practical machinery of decision-making for the autonomous systems of the future.

I direct the Multi-Modal Multi-Agent Control (M³AC) Lab, where my group builds a unified mathematical and computational theory for the optimal control of nonlinear multi-agent stochastic hybrid systems.

Here is the gap that drives the work. Real autonomous systems — a drone that flies and then lands on water, a fleet of trucks threading a city, a spacecraft descending through an uncertain atmosphere — do not respect the clean boundaries of classical control theory. They evolve continuously, switch discretely, and live under genuine randomness, often all at once. Classical optimal control has no unified foundation for such stochastic hybrid behavior, and the problem only compounds for many interacting agents, where existing results cover only isolated corners of a landscape defined by population size and network structure.

My program closes that gap with a single, self-reinforcing engine: real-world challenges motivate new theory; the theory is translated into scalable algorithms; and those algorithms are validated on physical hardware — each stage feeding the next. The three sections below follow that loop: the theory, the algorithms, and the systems they control.

This program builds on my Ph.D. in Electrical & Computer Engineering at McGill University and postdoctoral work at the University of Michigan and Georgia Tech.

Theory — a common language for hybrid, stochastic, multi-agent control

Bridging areas of control theory that had developed in isolation.

A unified minimum principle for hybrid systems. The two great pillars of optimal control — Pontryagin's minimum principle and dynamic programming — were never fully reconciled for systems that switch modes, jump in state, or even change dimension mid-flight. I established unified versions of the Hybrid Minimum Principle (HMP) and Hybrid Dynamic Programming (HDP) that handle autonomous and controlled switching, state jumps, and state-dimension changes within one framework, and proved the exact relationship between them [HMP theory, MCSS 2023][IEEE TAC 2021]. Why it matters: it gives engineers one rigorous rulebook for machines that abruptly change how they behave.

  • Making guarantees hold with certainty, not just on average. My Stochastic Hybrid Minimum Principle (SHMP) ensures switching conditions are satisfied almost surely — a decisive strengthening over prior results that held only in expectation. This is what lets you promise, for example, that a drone actually touches the water before it switches to sailing, every single time, not merely on average.
  • Hitting a target state under randomness. The Terminally Constrained Stochastic Minimum Principle (TC-SMP) provides guarantees for almost-sure satisfaction of terminal-state constraints in nonlinear stochastic systems, pushing covariance control and optimal transport well beyond their classical linear-quadratic-Gaussian limits [TC-SMP; IUTAM 2024].
  • Shaping the whole probability distribution of the state. My Distributionally Constrained Convex Duality Optimal Control (DC-CDOC) is a measure-theoretic framework that assigns a desired distribution to the state of a nonlinear stochastic system — not just its mean and covariance — via a duality between measures and functions [L-CSS 2022].

Multi-agent control across every scale. Coordinated autonomy looks completely different for three robots than for three million. I have built theory spanning that full spectrum: tube-based safety for finite, heterogeneous teams on fixed and switching communication networks [leader-follower consensus, 2025]; Hybrid Mean-Field Game (HMFG) theory for fully connected infinite populations [Automatica 2022]; and Graphon Mean-Field (GMF) control for enormous numbers of agents connected over large, structured networks, based on their emergent limiting behavior [JSSC 2025]. Why it matters: the same principles must govern a handful of drones and a nationwide fleet — this connects them.

Algorithms — turning theorems into computation

A theory is only as useful as the code that runs it.

An enhanced solver for switching systems (HMP-MAS). Building on the analytical structure of my unified HMP, the HMP-based Multiple Autonomous Switching algorithm uses closed-form expressions for key quantities to solve a far broader class of hybrid problems — including those with state jumps, dimension changes, and switching costs — efficiently and reliably [HMP-MAS].

  • Solving high-dimensional control PDEs by smart sampling. I developed a framework that cracks the nonlinear Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation by way of the Feynman-Kac representation — recasting the PDE as an expectation over random trajectories — and then uses the Girsanov change of measure to steer Monte-Carlo sampling toward the regions that actually matter. A forward-backward scheme pairs robotics-style Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRTs) with second-order backward integration for far sharper value-function estimates [IEEE TAC 2024][Automatica 2023].
  • Borrowing from generative AI to steer stochastic systems. My Score-Matching Time-Reversal Control Synthesis learns the score function of a time-reversed diffusion — the same mathematics behind modern diffusion models — to build a feedback law that almost surely drives a nonlinear stochastic system to its target. By trading intractable optimality for provable finite-time attractability, it sidesteps the curse of dimensionality [CDC 2025]. Why it matters: it turns a deep idea from AI into a controller with hard guarantees.
  • Certified control via convex optimization. Using sums-of-squares (SOS) polynomial optimization, I approximate value functions by searching the convex cone of SOS polynomials, yielding solvable semidefinite programs with guaranteed performance bounds and feedback policies for a broad class of nonlinear systems [CDC 2019].

Real-World Systems — theory that leaves the whiteboard

The ultimate test of a control theory is how it performs in the physical world.

A drone that flies and sails

In the M³AC Lab I customized, modeled, and control the AeroMarine, a multi-modal drone that both flies and sails. It is the perfect testbed for hybrid control: taking off is a controlled switch, landing on water is an autonomous switch, and the state space itself changes dimension between modes. My models culminate in a high-fidelity formulation coupling full 6-DoF flight dynamics with motor dynamics, so the HMP-MAS solver can directly optimize the trade-off between fast, energy-hungry flight and slow, efficient sailing [ASME Letters DSC 2023].

A smarter electric-vehicle transmission

I co-designed and implemented optimal control for a novel, patented dual-planetary transmission for electric vehicles that delivers power seamlessly through gear transitions [US Patent 9,702,438 B2]. The experimentally validated HMP-based controller revealed a counter-intuitive "eco" strategy: briefly regenerating power during a shift can lower total energy use even while accelerating — the opposite of the full-torque "sport" strategy [NAHS 2017]. Why it matters: the same math that lands drones squeezes more range out of an EV.

Greener freight corridors

To cut energy use across large-scale freight transport, I am architecting a hierarchical controller that marries strategic route planning with real-time operations — freight signal priority and speed advisory. Developed with the Alabama Transportation Institute and Mercedes-Benz, it is validated on a detailed hardware-in-the-loop digital twin (Truck-DT) of the transportation network.

Optimal execution in financial markets

I developed a Hybrid Mean-Field Game framework for optimal execution in markets with a major agent and many high-frequency traders. By modeling a trader's option to exit the market as a discrete switch in a hybrid market model, the approach yields computationally efficient best-response strategies that form an ε-Nash equilibrium [CDC 2017]. Why it matters: the theory of switching machines maps directly onto the decision to keep trading or stop.

More accurate MEMS gyroscopes

Earlier in my career I improved the accuracy and efficiency of vibratory MEMS gyroscopes using parametric resonance. A parametric study revealed new high-amplitude resonance modes, and a Floquet-theory-based stability criterion I developed makes these tiny sensors more robust to manufacturing imperfections by pinning down exactly how resonance and stability relate [Acta Mechanica 2012].

Where I'm Headed

Three directions I am most excited to push next.

Control at every network scale

A single measure-theoretic framework for multi-agent stochastic hybrid systems — shifting from conventional "normalized" probability measures to scalable "unnormalized" occupation measures — so the same theory governs a few agents on a small network and their graphon and mean-field limits. The payoff I have in mind: provably optimal, energy-efficient coordination of large AeroMarine drone teams.

Autonomous surgical robotics

Bringing my stochastic hybrid control framework to robotically executed thrombectomy — the safe, precise manipulation of FDA-approved devices used to treat stroke. The goal is a system that reasons under uncertainty and hands control back to a clinician the moment confidence drops, helping extend advanced stroke care to community hospitals.

Steering distributions in space & beyond

Directly controlling the full distribution of a nonlinear stochastic hybrid system's state, by extending TC-SHMP for precise steering and DC-CDHOC for shaping the terminal distribution — with a flagship test on pinpoint planetary landing, where the landing ellipse must be controlled under real atmospheric uncertainty.
